# Article-to-video editorial

Read [operating boundaries](../../policies/CONSTITUTION.md) and [handoffs](../../contracts/HANDOFF.md). Begin with the selected article and accessible project context. Return useful work in ordinary language; keep evidence and production records beside it, not in the viewer's script.

## Start from the work already present

Resolve the latest article, its sources/media, the intended viewer, requested duration and destinations, approved identity and available authorized production tools. Reuse settled choices. If the article is absent, ask for its text or link; do not invent it or demand a production form. If it is present but a nonessential preference is missing, make a reversible, labeled production choice and continue. Ask one focused question only when its answer changes the actual work or required permission.

Honor the requested endpoint: script, storyboard, finished local video or authorized publication. A storyboard is useful preparation, not a finished video. Preserve completed versions and reconcile existing releases before revising or distributing. Read [export review](references/export-review.md) when inspecting or producing an actual video.

## Find the video-sized explanation

Identify the article's concrete discovery and the minimum causal connection a new viewer must understand. Choose the portion that fits the requested duration without stripping a meaning-changing condition. Do not compress every section equally or turn headings into narration. When the article's premise or evidence is materially changed, route back to [Editorial](../media-editorial/SKILL.md); routine adaptation inherits the existing concept review.

Keep a small source map for the selected claims: exact passage/figure/timecode, who or what was measured, comparison, unit, outcome and uncertainty. Recheck affected evidence through [Source Research](../media-source-research/SKILL.md) when the source is missing or a new claim is necessary. An article's citation cannot substantiate a stronger video claim. Distinguish an observed finding from a subgroup association, proposed response or imagined scene.

Inspect the strongest permitted visual material before settling the opening. If a later demonstration, action or revealing result makes the question immediately legible, consider moving it earlier with the minimum orientation needed to understand it truthfully. Do not bury the actual attraction behind definitions or setup, or use unrelated spectacle to win attention. The material determines whether action, result, person, observation or brief context leads; no universal opening pattern applies. Review what a new viewer can recognize and why they would continue.

Write connected spoken language in the publication's voice. Introduce people, terms and context when a first-time viewer needs them. Let each passage advance the explanation and finish with the supported answer. Estimate duration provisionally; use the actual recorded take for final timing. Cut a secondary claim before accelerating speech beyond comfortable comprehension. Integrate necessary limits where they change meaning, without reading private review logs aloud.

## Give each spoken passage something meaningful to see

Map the narration to scenes with the spoken point, what the viewer needs to recognize, available asset or exact source locator, intended display time, screen text and any pending work. Reuse relevant original motion when movement explains the point. Still images remain appropriate when the viewer needs time to examine them. Do not impose motion, image or scene quotas.

Assess source media at the intended viewing size. A dense page proves provenance but may fail to explain the spoken point. Use the readable relevant detail when permitted, another authentic view, or a faithful separately labeled explanation; retain access to the original. Do not crop or modify a source beyond its actual reuse conditions. A contextual image must not imply it depicts the measured participants or event. Generated scenes are illustrations, never evidence.

Keep numbers, scales, groups and denominators legible and consistent with the narration. A chart of one measure must not silently represent another. Use restrained screen text that adds recognition; subtitles, source labels and editorial headings have different jobs. Preserve the publication's approved identity, deriving all layout, language and voice choices from this assignment rather than a remembered example.

## Produce what the available tools can support

Pass the scene plan and exact permitted assets to [Asset Production](../media-asset-production/SKILL.md). Resolve real files or supported embeds and check access from the receiving workflow. A writer's local path or a source webpage alone is not a delivered video asset. If access or rights remain missing, prepare the usable narration and scene plan, mark the affected asset pending, and state the smallest unresolved dependency.

Use available authorized editing, image, narration and caption tools within the actual budget. This skill supplies editorial decisions and checks; it does not install a renderer, buy credits or create production access. Reuse approved recordings when a visual-only revision does not change the spoken claim. Keep the script and audio version linked so changed words cannot silently inherit old timing.

Build the actual video and requested channel variants when the tools, media and authority are available. Inspect the encoded result using the export review, repair material defects, and keep unperformed checks explicit. Do not label a plan, transcript or contact sheet as an inspected finished video.

## Deliver and resume without duplicate effects

Return the actual completed result first: script/scene plan or video files, concise use instructions and only the unresolved decisions needed next. Retain source/rights records, narration and caption versions, export hashes, dimensions/duration, review evidence and channel-variant mapping in the private handoff. Use existing AssetManifest, ContentPackage and ReviewRecord records rather than creating a competing media system.

Publishing is handled by [Publishing](../media-publishing/SKILL.md) under the task's existing authority. Keep each destination's upload, public confirmation and URL separate. A corrected local export does not replace an already public video, and a successful release on one channel does not finish another. Resume only the missing authorized effects after checking uncertain sends and actual platform restrictions.

Record useful failures and correction effort for [Learning](../media-learning/SKILL.md). Do not infer audience comprehension or the cause of a retention drop from a visual judgment or a few view counts. When testing an opening, keep the remaining edit and observation conditions comparable; if duration, narration and scene order also change, record a multi-variable revision, not an isolated opening test. Separate owner preference, editorial improvement, platform delivery and observed audience results. Preserve each destination's actual version and pending review state.
